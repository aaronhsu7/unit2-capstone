# Spoonful Information Security Policy

Policy ID: SEC-001
Owner: Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
Applies to: All employees, contractors, and third parties with access to Spoonful systems
Last reviewed: March 2025 (reviewed annually)

## About this policy

This policy describes how Spoonful protects company and customer data. It sets the minimum security requirements that every person with access to Spoonful systems must follow. Team-specific standards (for example, the Code Review Process and the Incident Response Runbook) build on this policy and may add stricter requirements, but they may never relax it.

Violations of this policy may result in loss of system access and disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or contract.

## Data classification

All Spoonful data falls into one of four classification levels. The owner of a dataset is responsible for labelling it.

1. Public: Information approved for public release, such as marketing pages and published documentation.
2. Internal: Information intended for Spoonful staff only, such as internal wikis and meeting notes. This is the default level if no label is applied.
3. Confidential: Business-sensitive information, such as financial results, sales pipeline data, employee records, and source code.
4. Restricted: Customer data, authentication secrets, encryption keys, and payment information. Restricted data has the strictest handling rules.

Restricted data must never be copied to personal devices, pasted into unapproved third-party tools (including public AI chat tools), or shared over chat. It may only be stored in systems approved by the Security team.

## Access control

Access to Spoonful systems follows the principle of least privilege: you receive only the access you need to do your job.

- Access requests are submitted through the IT service portal and must be approved by your manager and the system owner.
- Access to production systems and to Restricted data requires an additional approval from the Security team.
- Managers review their team's access every quarter. Access that is no longer needed must be removed within 5 business days of the review.
- When an employee leaves Spoonful, all access is revoked within 24 hours of their last working day. For involuntary departures, access is revoked immediately.
- Shared accounts are not permitted, except for documented service accounts owned by a named team.

## Authentication and passwords

- Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is mandatory for all company systems, including email, source control, cloud consoles, and the VPN. Hardware security keys are required for administrators.
- Passwords must be at least 14 characters long. Passphrases are encouraged.
- Passwords must be stored in the company-approved password manager. Do not reuse your Spoonful password on any other site.
- Passwords are not rotated on a fixed schedule, but must be changed immediately if there is any suspicion of compromise.
- API keys, tokens, and other secrets must be stored in the secrets manager. Secrets must never be committed to source control. Pre-commit secret scanning is enabled on all repositories, and a detected secret is treated as a security incident.

## Devices

- Company laptops must use full-disk encryption, automatic screen lock after 5 minutes of inactivity, and the endpoint protection agent installed by IT.
- Operating system security updates must be installed within 14 days of release. Critical updates must be installed within 72 hours.
- Personal devices may access email and chat through the managed mobile app only. Personal devices may not access source code, production systems, or Restricted data.
- Lost or stolen devices must be reported to security@spoonful.example within 1 hour of discovery so they can be remotely wiped.

## Network and remote work

- Access to internal systems from outside the office requires the company VPN or the zero-trust access gateway.
- Public Wi-Fi may be used only while connected to the VPN.
- Do not discuss Confidential or Restricted information in public places where you can be overheard.

## Third-party vendors

Before any vendor receives Confidential or Restricted data, the Security team must complete a vendor risk assessment. Vendors handling Restricted data must hold a current SOC 2 Type II report or ISO 27001 certification, and must sign Spoonful's data processing agreement. Vendor assessments are renewed every year.

## Reporting security incidents

If you suspect a security incident, report it immediately. You will never be penalised for reporting in good faith, even if it turns out to be a false alarm.

- Report through the #security-incidents channel or by emailing security@spoonful.example.
- Suspected incidents must be reported within 1 hour of discovery.
- Do not attempt to investigate or fix the issue yourself, and do not delete evidence such as suspicious emails.
- The handling of incidents after they are reported is described in the Incident Response Runbook.

## Security training

All staff must complete security awareness training within 14 days of joining Spoonful and again every year. Engineers must also complete secure coding training annually. The Security team runs a phishing simulation every quarter; staff who fail a simulation are assigned a short refresher module.

## Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy must be requested in writing from the Security team, must include a business justification and compensating controls, and are approved by the CISO. Exceptions are granted for a maximum of 6 months and must then be renewed or closed.
